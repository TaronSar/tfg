================================================================================
UAV IDENTITY VERIFICATION — PROJECT REPORT
Few-shot ProtoNet deployment pipeline for Embention
Date: 2026-06-29
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The goal was not a thesis-style analysis but a working, demonstrable product: a
system that verifies the identity of a UAV seen by a camera. The operational
model is deliberately simple and binary. A client enrolls a single target by
providing about five reference images. From that moment on, for every frame the
camera sees, the system answers exactly one yes/no question: does the UAV in this
frame match the enrolled target? It never ranks the target against a list of
known drones, and it never retrains for a new target. The identities used to
train the model are irrelevant at runtime — only the enrolled gallery matters.
This is the regime a deployed device on an aircraft would actually run in, and
everything below was built and measured against that regime.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT WE BUILT, AND WHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We assembled an end-to-end pipeline from existing pieces plus new glue code. The
chain is: a raw camera frame goes to the YOLOX detector, which finds the UAV and
crops it; the crop goes to the ProtoNet encoder, which turns it into a 128-number
embedding; that embedding is compared by cosine similarity to the enrolled
target's prototype; the score is thresholded into MATCH or UNKNOWN; and many
per-frame decisions are aggregated into one overall verdict. We chose this
structure because it mirrors deployment reality: detect, crop, embed, compare to
the one enrolled gallery, decide.

The components we created or hardened:

- src/deploy.py — the Verifier class. It loads the trained model and the enrolled
  gallery, embeds any crop, and produces the exact same match score the rest of
  the system uses. We made this the single source of truth so calibration and
  deployment can never disagree on how a score is computed.

- src/enroll.py — turns five reference images into a tiny gallery file (~2 KB),
  the only artifact that ever leaves the client, which matters for a privacy-
  sensitive deployment.

- scripts/calibrate_threshold.py — feeds positive crops (the real target) and
  negative crops (impostors) through the scorer and recommends a decision
  threshold, reporting detection rate, false-accept rate, and accuracy. It exists
  so the threshold is chosen from data rather than guessed.

- scripts/deploy_verify.py — the one-command demo. It accepts either a video file
  or a folder of frames, runs the full pipeline, writes a per-frame CSV, and
  prints a verdict. Extras: crop caching (re-scoring against a new gallery skips
  re-extraction), annotated overlays, Grad-CAM heatmaps, and a median-score gate.

- scripts/plot_embedding_map.py — visualizes the embedding space: 2D/3D
  projections and a full-128D pairwise similarity matrix, coloured by identity,
  so cluster quality can be seen rather than guessed.

Verdict rule: CONFIRMED if >=3 matches AND >=25% of detected frames clear the
threshold AND the median frame score clears it. The median gate was added so an
impostor cannot pass merely by grazing the bar on a minority of frames; the
median describes the typical frame and ignores lucky outliers.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT WE DID, STEP BY STEP, AND WHAT WE OBTAINED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
First, we validated the happy path. We enrolled a demo target and ran a real
video of it through the pipeline. The genuine target was CONFIRMED and an
impostor video was correctly rejected. This proved the plumbing worked end to end
and produced sensible decisions.

Then we stress-tested with several real UAV videos (skywalker, hornet, vector,
lancet) scored against several galleries, and the results split sharply in two:
  - Against a clearly DIFFERENT drone (lancet), separation was clean: the true
    target's typical frame scored about +0.31 while the impostor sat at -0.25, a
    healthy gap of roughly 0.56.
  - Against SIMILAR-looking drones (hornet, vector), the system failed. Impostors
    averaged 0.46 against the skywalker target while the true target averaged
    0.41 — the impostors scored HIGHER. Calibration confirmed a balanced accuracy
    of 51.8%, essentially a coin flip.

Next, we caught a misleading summary metric. The original headline reported the
average of the top-15 frames, which cherry-picks outliers and made impostors look
close to the target. We replaced it with the median frame score and the percent
of frames above threshold — robust numbers that describe the typical frame, not
the luckiest one.

We then noticed the verdict could still be fooled: an impostor gallery confirmed
with 40% of frames over threshold but a median of only 0.12. We added the median
gate, which turned that false confirmation into NOT CONFIRMED while keeping the
true target confirmed.

The Grad-CAM heatmaps then revealed the root cause: the model's attention was on
the sky, not the airframe. We tested this directly by removing the background. The
effect was dramatic — the true target jumped from 0.34 to 0.69 and a similar
impostor dropped from 0.71 to 0.49. With sky present the true target had actually
scored LOWEST of all galleries; with sky gone, the ranking corrected. This proved
the encoder was heavily keying on background.

We tried to fix background at training time with a colour-based mask, but it
chewed into the small UAVs and degraded quality, so we reverted it. The honest
conclusion: a clean fix needs pixel-perfect masks (Blender alpha), not a
heuristic on baked JPGs.

Finally, we added real-world images and retrained (42 train / 15 val identities,
mixed-domain enrollment, euclidean metric, degrade augmentation). We obtained a
best validation accuracy of 0.811 with training accuracy at 1.000 — a ~19-point
gap that signals overfitting to training identities, with real generalization
sitting around 0.81.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT THESE RESULTS MEAN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The pipeline is sound and demonstrable: enroll once, verify per frame, reject
  impostors that are clearly different. Aggregating across many frames makes the
  verdict robust to occasional bad frames.
- The encoder partly identifies "sky" rather than "this airframe." That confound
  inflates impostor scores whenever the sky matches and is the single biggest
  cheap-to-address weakness.
- The encoder cannot tell apart UAVs that genuinely look alike at small crop
  sizes; their embeddings collapse together. No threshold, gate, or aggregation
  can separate what the embeddings do not encode — a representation limit, not a
  tuning problem.
- The train/val gap means the model leans on memorized synthetic identities; real
  performance is ~0.81. Defensible claim: reliable against distinct UAVs, not yet
  against look-alikes at operational resolution.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT WE SHOULD DO TO GET BETTER RESULTS (BY IMPACT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Hard-negative training: teach the encoder using similar UAVs as explicit
   negatives so it learns the fine differences. Highest leverage on the core
   look-alike failure.
2. Background handling: keep enrollment background-removed (the winning config)
   and apply size-conditional background removal to queries above ~80px while
   skipping tiny crops; generate clean masks via Blender alpha, not heuristics.
3. More real frames per identity: natural background variety teaches invariance;
   never duplicate the same crop with/without background (that is memorization).
4. Reduce overfitting: keep degrade augmentation, deploy best.pth not last.pth,
   add regularization or early stopping.
5. Resolution gate: for crops below ~40px, report DETECTED but IDENTITY-UNCERTAIN
   instead of a confident verdict, since identity is not recoverable that small.

Single highest-impact action: fine-tune on real UAV crops with hard negatives.
